# X

Keep the post understandable without opening a thread. Short paragraphs, one useful link. No hashtags, decorative emoji, generic questions, or launch hype unless the user asks.

For a release post, name the version, what changed, and one concrete measure of scope. Summarize the result instead of pasting a changelog.

## Launch media

- Look at the live brand website or current brand source before designing the asset. Do not guess the brand from generic launch conventions.
- Reuse the brand's typography, spacing, colors, and familiar visual elements. Prefer a text-focused card or a real product screenshot over a collage of interfaces.
- Give a single launch image one headline, at most one supporting line, and one visual motif. It may identify the release, but it should not repeat the caption.
- Check readability at feed size. Remove fake interface labels, browser chrome, tiny copy, grids, glow, gradients, and extra decoration unless they belong to the brand.
- For a single landscape asset, 16:9 is a good default. Keep essential copy away from the outer crop area.
- For media made on a remote machine, give the user preview and download links that open in a browser. A temporary server on the Tailscale network works for review.
