# Commercial scoping

Answer the pricing question directly. A small addition may need only an amount and short explanation; a substantial quote needs a supported calculation. Use the existing agreement to choose the billing basis.

Read only the relevant guidance: [fixed-price.md](references/fixed-price.md), [hourly.md](references/hourly.md), or [recurring.md](references/recurring.md). For historical comparisons or several sources, use [evidence-sources.md](references/evidence-sources.md).

Use the rate confirmed in this project's accepted estimate, contract, or direct agreement. Do not reuse a rate from another project unless it has been confirmed here. Use Productive hours only when Sil explicitly asks for them.

Before pricing additional work, check what is already included, billed, completed, or reusable. For a defect, check the agreed scope and support terms before deciding whether the correction is covered or billable. Do not promise a free fix or add a charge without evidence. Separate broader requests from the existing commitment.

When revising a price already presented, preserve that version, source, and presentation time when known. Explain major changes and distinguish proposed, approved, and customer-presented amounts. Keep calculation notes with the existing scope.

Lead with the recommended amount or change, what it covers, and any uncertainty that affects the price. Compare with verified past work and adjust for reuse, supplied designs, data readiness, and integration uncertainty. Make underpricing visible and charge shared costs only once.

Use `customer-communication` for customer-facing wording, `moneybird` to operate quotes and invoices, and `work-management` when the agreement changes tracked work.
