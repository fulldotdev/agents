# Commercial scoping

Answer the actual pricing question. A small addition may need only a recommended amount and a short explanation; a substantial quote needs a supported calculation. Use the existing agreement to choose the billing basis, rather than assuming every job is fixed-price.

Read only the relevant guidance: [fixed-price.md](references/fixed-price.md), [hourly.md](references/hourly.md), or [recurring.md](references/recurring.md). For historical comparisons or several sources, use [evidence-sources.md](references/evidence-sources.md).

Use the engagement's verified rate from its accepted estimate, contract or direct agreement. Never carry a rate across engagements by default. Use Productive hours only when Sil explicitly asks for them.

Check what is already included, billed, completed or reusable before pricing additional work. For a defect, check the agreed scope and support terms before deciding whether the correction is covered or chargeable. Do not automatically promise a free fix or invent an extra charge. Separate broader requests from the existing commitment.

When revising a presented price, preserve that version, its source and presentation time when known. Explain material changes and distinguish proposed, approved and customer-presented amounts. Keep calculation notes with the existing scope; do not create a separate document for every small decision.

Lead with the recommended amount or change, what it covers, and any uncertainty that materially affects it. Use verified past work as a comparison, adjusted for reuse, supplied designs, data readiness and integration uncertainty. Do not hide underpricing in vague wording or charge a shared cost twice.

Use `customer-communication` for customer-facing wording, `moneybird` to operate quotes and invoices, and `work-management` when the agreement changes tracked work.
