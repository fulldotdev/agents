# Design

Make the requested task clear and easy to complete. Follow the existing product, brand, design system, supplied design, and real content. Use relevant Figma or component skills for their tools; this skill defines design judgment, not a new toolchain.

## Simplicity

- Give each screen or section a clear purpose and primary action. Use hierarchy, spacing, typography, and grouping before adding containers or decoration.
- Reuse established components and patterns. Add a variant or interaction only when the current task needs it; prefer fewer steps and choices over more explanation.
- Every element must support understanding, navigation, action, trust, or a required state. Remove task-added filler instead of polishing it.
- Avoid generic decoration: gratuitous gradients, badges, eyebrows, icon tiles, nested cards, oversized headings, repeated calls to action, and ornamental motion. Preserve these when they are intentional parts of the supplied design or brand.
- Use concrete labels and truthful content. Do not invent testimonials, statistics, customer logos, functionality, or extra sections to fill a layout. Mockup placeholders must be recognizable as placeholders.

## Scope and review

Resolve the actual usability or visual problem. A small correction is not permission to redesign the page, rewrite its content, introduce a design system, or add features. Suggest a broader change separately when it would materially help; otherwise finish the requested work.

Preserve keyboard access, readable contrast, labels, and useful empty, loading, or error states where relevant. Simplicity must not hide necessary information or functionality.

Inspect the result at the screen sizes and states affected by the change. Check alignment, spacing, text wrapping, hierarchy, and the primary action. Remove additions that do not earn their place. Use the existing preview or artifact for review; do not create a separate presentation or report unless requested.
