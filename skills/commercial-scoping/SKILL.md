# Commercial scoping

Answer the pricing question directly: the recommended amount, what it covers, and any uncertainty that affects the price. A small addition needs an amount and a short explanation. A substantial quote needs a calculation you can show. Take the billing basis from the existing agreement.

## Fixed price

This is the default. Group outcomes into categories with one customer price each, and list the smaller deliverables under each category without their own prices. Optional work gets its own price.

- Each price includes implementation, coordination, feedback, testing, and release. Put risk into the affected category, not into a generic contingency.
- Keep hours, the rate, and the calculation internal. Customer scopes are in euros and end at the total.
- Offer versions or options only when there is a real choice. Do not add a smaller variant to an agreed scope.
- Check the existing implementation and price the remaining change, not a rebuild.
- For websites: a **layout** is a complete page or route, a **block** is a full-width content section, a **component** is a smaller reusable element.
- For work billed in parts, invoice each part as `termijn 1/2` with the Statement of Work or quote in the reference.

## Hourly and recurring

Hourly: use the agreed rate, show hours and rate, and keep estimate, approved limit, and actual hours apart. An estimate is not approval to spend it.

Recurring: start from the current agreement. Keep the fixed fee apart from reserved hours and one-off setup. For a package change, give what changes, the new amount, and the start date. The old terms hold until approved.

## Evidence

Use the rate from this project's accepted estimate, contract, or direct agreement, not one from another project. Before pricing extra work, check what is already included, billed, done, or reusable. For a defect, check the agreed scope and support terms before deciding whether the fix is covered.

Compare with past work you have verified, and adjust for reuse, supplied designs, data readiness, and integration risk. When sources disagree, trust the current implementation and written agreement first, then a recent explicit decision, then invoices and delivered work, then older estimates. Make underpricing visible. Charge shared costs once.

When revising a price that was already presented, keep that version, its source, and its date. Keep proposed, approved, and customer-presented amounts apart.

Use `customer-communication` for customer-facing wording, `moneybird` for quotes and invoices, and `work-management` when the agreement changes tracked work.
